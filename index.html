    What this is

    This is not a video. Everything you see is being drawn by your browser, right now, as the song plays: the rain, the wet avenue and its mirror, the lamps, the hands, the car that switches lanes at you. The words are built as solid letters and placed in the street at the exact moment they are sung. Walk the same song twice and the city is the same, but you are not.

    Hyperfeeling

    I have what I call a hypermind. It does not idle. It runs every thought to the end and then past it, and it does the same with feeling: nothing arrives at normal volume. Joy comes in too loud, betrayal too deep, loyalty too total. For years I treated that as a fault to be managed. These songs are what happened when I stopped managing it and started writing it down exactly as it feels — hypered, enhanced, unapologetic. A hyperfeeling song is an emotion at its real size.

    How a song is made here

    The words are mine, written in the hours when the mind will not stop. The music is produced with AI, from my direction, in the noir British trip-hop that fits a city at 3 a.m. Then the words come back to me as a place: I decide where each line stands, what it does, what the street does to it — and I build it with code, as a world, not as a film. If you have ever wanted to stand inside a lyric, this is that.

    The years

    The last years took more than they gave. People I had held up turned around and watched me fall; promises came with expiry dates I was not told about; there were months of silence where I could not find the words at all. I lost things I thought were mine to keep, and a version of myself I thought was permanent. What I found instead is in these songs: that the silence ends, that the words come back late but they come back, and that the man standing alone on the pavement is still standing.

    Me

    I am direct. I am loyal to a fault and I notice everything — both are the hypermind. I build things: tours you can walk through, tools that read a room, songs you can stand inside. I believe karma keeps books. I believe faith is what you do on the bad nights, not what you say on the good ones. And I would rather say the true thing too loudly than the comfortable thing at the right volume.
